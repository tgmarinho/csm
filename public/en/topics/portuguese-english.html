Languages

Care in Portuguese and English

Care can happen in Portuguese or English, respecting the language in which you feel more comfortable expressing yourself.

Some people access their history, bonds and emotional language more naturally in Portuguese. Others, because of routine, school, work or life context, can organize certain experiences more clearly in English. The choice of language can be part of care.

When language matters

Language is not only translation. It influences the way a person names emotions, describes situations, thinks, feels shame or asks for help. In therapy, being able to choose or move between Portuguese and English can make communication more spontaneous and precise.

My background also includes experience with Brazilian children and teenagers who speak Portuguese, but who, because they live in another cultural or school context, feel more comfortable expressing themselves in English. In these cases, being able to move between the two languages becomes an important difference: listening can follow the language the person chooses to name what they feel, while still respecting their history, family and cultural references.

Who this is for

  • People who want to speak in Portuguese about history, family and bonds.
  • People who feel more comfortable organizing experiences in English.
  • Brazilian families in bilingual contexts who notice this movement between languages.

Frequently asked questions about languages

Can sessions happen in English?

Yes. Sessions can be held in English when that is the best form of communication for the patient.

Can I move between Portuguese and English?

Yes. When it makes sense for the process, the language can be agreed on or alternated according to how you feel more comfortable expressing yourself.

Can this support people in bilingual contexts?

It may help when someone moves between Portuguese and English at school, work, with family or socially, and needs listening that is sensitive to that experience.
